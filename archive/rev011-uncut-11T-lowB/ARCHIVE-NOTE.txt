Rev 011 (2026-08-10) — DESIGN OF RECORD. Forked from Rev 009 (uncut rim, 11T).

WHY: the owner wanted more belt on the ground without buying a longer belt.
The 1080 mm loop was being spent 265 sprocket wrap / 226 idler wraps / 386
diagonals / 204 ground. Analysis showed (a) the isoceles triangle already
maximises the base for a given apex height, so no layout change helps, and
(b) the diagonals are governed by B. Lowering B converts diagonal into ground.

THE CHANGE: B 170 -> 150 (vehicle sits 20 mm lower; the track conversion had
raised it ~120 mm over the stock wheel, so this gives a little back and does
not affect belt-to-fender clearance, which the sprocket sets).
  ground contact  203.5 -> 231.2 mm  (+13.6%)
  ground pressure ~0.27 -> ~0.24 kg/cm2 at ~130 kg all-up
  wheel travel    +27.6/-25.0 -> +30.7/-29.2

CASCADE (all re-solved, all guards PASS, zero warnings):
  drop 38 -> 22    B=150 pushes the pivot toward the sprocket; 22 is what the
                   arm-corner clearance allows.
  theta 72 -> 80   the flatter arm (na 20.5 -> 10.8 deg) had rotated the shock
                   back over the carrier; 80 restores an upright shock.
  a_frac -> 0.434  keeps MR at 0.4351 = the purchased springs' design point.
  arm rear corners CHAMFERED 10x10 (new fabrication step).
  cut lengths: carrier 220, trailing arm 184, leading arm 166; shock hole at
  51.1 from the pivot; upper shock eye at (+-52, +2).

NEW GUARD: "arm rear corner to sprocket swept rib" — sweeps the chamfered
corner over the full travel. This collision was UNGUARDED in every earlier
revision; Rev 009 sits at 4.1 mm, Rev 011 at 5.8 mm. Worst case is full droop,
and the trailing arm's inner plate is the one at risk (teeth reach |z|=25.5,
that plate sits at |z|=25.4).

UNCHANGED: the sprocket (11T, rib 18x16.54, wheel O198, cord O210) and all its
print files/STLs; every purchased item (M12 GB901 8.8 stud axles + O15x12
sleeves, shocks 150/100kg-8.5, 6302 bearings, tube stock, bronze bushings);
the idler wheels (O108, tread 20.27 riding in the belt's 22 mm lug gap);
keel still deleted; carriers still keel-hole-free.

2026-08-11 — 1:1 DRILLING TEMPLATES REISSUED (rev011_1to1_drilling_templates.pdf)

Symptom: the leading arm bar printed ~153 mm against its own 165.7 callout.

Cause was NOT the drawing. The old sheet's artwork measured 165.698 x 40.000 mm
in the file, exact to 0.002 mm. It was laid out full-bleed on A4, and a
full-bleed page is larger than any printer's printable area, so "Scale to Fit"
(Preview's default) and "Shrink oversized pages" (Acrobat's default) both scale
it down 6-9% without saying so. 165.7 x ~0.92 = ~153.

Fix: the sheet is now generated by make_1to1_templates.py onto a 195 x 259.5 mm
page — smaller than the printable area of BOTH A4 and Letter, so nothing is
oversized and those two defaults become no-ops. One part per page, each page a
single SVG whose unit is 1 mm (part and ruler cannot scale apart), each with an
X and a Y scale check plus a reprint-percentage table. The generator re-opens
its own PDF and asserts every outline, hole and ruler before exiting.

Every part and hole is byte-identical to the old sheet EXCEPT one correction:
the carrier's hub-axle key was drawn as a slot with flats 9.9 apart. The SCAD
(apollo_track_pod_rev011.scad:641-642: circle(d=10.4) intersected with
square([10.4, 8.9])), plates_rev011.dxf polylines 3/4, and the old sheet's OWN
callout all say Ø10.4 with flats 8.9 apart and NO slot. It is drawn that way
now. This is the torque-arm key — a slot there lets the axle rotate.
